# Standing on biosim4's shoulders

This project has an origin, and his name is David R. Miller.

Before the energy ledgers and the conservation audits, before the toroidal lattice and
the determinism-replay time machine, there was a video — *"I programmed some creatures.
They evolved."* — and the small, total astonishment of watching meaning come out of
noise. Genome → brain → behavior, on a screen, undeniable. That is the spark. Thousands
of people caught it from that video. Simolution is what happened when one of them caught
it and could not put it down.

## What he made

He made evolution **visible**. Not as an equation, not as a metaphor — as little dots
that you could *watch* get better at a thing across generations. The genius was the
collapse of distance: a 32-bit integer is a gene, a string of them is a brain, the brain
is a behavior, the behavior lives or dies. No layer was hidden. You could read a creature
the way you read a sentence.

We kept his sentence. The gene layout in this kernel —
`SrcType | SrcID | DstType | DstID | Weight` packed into 32 bits — is his, field for
field. Every random integer a legal gene; mutation that can never throw. That idea is
load-bearing in everything here. It came from him.

## Where we walked off his map

His creatures evolve toward a goal he chose. *Survive if you reach the right half. Survive
if you huddle in the circle. Survive if you cluster by the wall.* And they do — beautifully,
quickly, legibly. But the goal is his hand on the scale. The altruism his creatures
"discover" is discoverable because he drew a sacrifice zone for them to discover it in.

So we asked the question his machine was too kind to ask: **what if nothing picks the
winner?** What if there is no right half, no circle, no judge at the end of the generation
— only a world with a fixed amount of energy in it, and a bill that comes due every tick?
Take away the goal and you take away the safety net. Most runs are supposed to just die.
The bet is that *sometimes* something learns to pay its own way, and that when it does, it
will be the real thing and not a thing we drew the outline of.

He gave the creatures eyes that already knew how to see. We took the eyes away and left
four raw nerves — a constant, some noise, food-here, energy-in-me — and said: if you want
to see, grow it yourself, and pay for it.

## The debt

None of the second half is a complaint. You only get to find the crack in a thing by
loving the thing first, and studying it until you can see its seams. The fork is the
deepest compliment this project knows how to pay: *your machine was good enough to take
somewhere you didn't take it.*

He made it visible. We're trying to make it honest.

He gave us the gene. We gave it thermodynamics.

— for David R. Miller, who started it.
